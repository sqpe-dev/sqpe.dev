# Project standards

## Editorial voice

Write in the maintainer's academic style. Treat `references/state-of-the-art.md` as research input, not finished prose or an authority to reproduce uncritically. Favor precise definitions, clear taxonomy, substantive comparisons, and direct explanations. Use plain language where it is accurate. Distinguish evidence, interpretation, limitations, and open questions.

Avoid promotional slogans, rhetorical contrasts, inflated claims, generic enthusiasm, repetitive caveats, and formulaic introductions or conclusions. Every paragraph should convey information. Do not invent citations, credentials, authorship claims, review work, benchmark results, or guarantees.

## Project identity

Keep the project focused on its subject and contributors. Do not add assistant or development-tool branding, generated-by notices, production-process boilerplate, or tool attribution to website content, documentation, commit messages, pull requests, release notes, or assets. These standards apply to all subsequent work.

Keep scholarly citations faithful to their sources. Do not alter a paper's title to satisfy a branding preference; resolve whether to retain or exclude the citation instead.

## Evidence

Verify claims against primary sources. Separate publication-record review, full-paper review, implementation inspection, and independent reproduction. Preserve specific limitations without repeating generic disclaimers. Attribute vendor claims and distinguish them from independent evidence. Never present proposed work as completed.

## Design

Use restrained academic typography, readable spacing, clear information hierarchy, and a neutral palette. Avoid decorative gradients, fashionable accent colors, stock motifs, unnecessary animations, and marketing-style layouts. Visuals should explain the subject or help readers navigate it.

## Delivery

Run the build and relevant checks. Inspect responsive layouts after visual changes. Keep deployment configuration and documentation consistent with the actual production setup. Never claim a release is live without verifying deployment.
